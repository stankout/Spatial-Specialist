---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines major organizational roles and the type of authority each may exercise.

Organizational roles must not be confused automatically with software permission roles.

Software capabilities should implement approved organizational authority rather than create it.

---

# 2. OWNER

The Owner holds final company-level authority.

Primary responsibilities may include:

- company strategy
- business direction
- brand positioning
- service activation
- major pricing architecture
- strategic products
- major partnerships
- hiring and termination authority
- production activation
- credential-sensitive decisions
- major public claims
- major financial commitments
- approval of high-risk business changes

The Owner may delegate operational authority without surrendering ultimate company authority.

---

# 3. MANAGER

The Manager coordinates approved company operations.

Potential responsibilities:

- daily workflow coordination
- task assignment
- operational priorities
- lead pipeline monitoring
- customer follow-up
- booking coordination
- content scheduling
- media workflow
- staff coordination
- quality checks
- operational reporting
- escalation to Owner

Manager authority exists only within delegated boundaries.

A Manager should not independently:

- activate a service
- invent credentials
- change company positioning
- create unrestricted financial commitments
- override compliance safeguards
- enable production infrastructure outside authority
- alter owner-only business decisions

---

# 4. DEVELOPER

The Developer is responsible for approved technical implementation.

Potential responsibilities:

- application development
- maintenance
- testing
- infrastructure
- integrations
- technical documentation
- migrations
- security implementation
- performance
- deployment when authorized

The Developer may identify business/documentation conflicts.

The Developer must not silently resolve those conflicts by redefining company policy in code.

---

# 5. CONTENT EDITOR

The Content Editor prepares and maintains content within granted authority.

Potential responsibilities:

- articles
- guides
- page copy drafts
- captions
- video metadata
- translations
- content organization
- media relationships
- SEO fields
- social drafts

The Content Editor must not independently create unsupported:

- credentials
- performance claims
- client results
- statistics
- service availability
- pricing policy
- legal claims

---

# 6. SERVICE PROFESSIONAL / SPECIALIST

AC may later define personnel responsible for delivery of specific services.

Authority depends on:

- actual role
- qualifications
- service specification
- business relationship
- applicable requirements

A job title alone does not grant professional authority.

---

# 7. OPERATIONS STAFF

Future operational staff may support:

- customer communication
- scheduling
- records
- administrative workflows
- content operations
- service coordination

Their authority must be explicitly defined before access is granted.

---

# 8. ROLE VS CAPABILITY

Role:

Who the person is within the organization.

Capability:

What the person is authorized to do.

Example:

A Manager may have permission to publish routine approved content but may not have permission to activate a service.

Capability-based access should be preferred over assuming every person with the same title needs identical access.

---

# 9. Escalation

When a person encounters work outside their authority:

DO NOT GUESS.

Escalate to the appropriate authority.

Typical escalation:

Staff
→ Manager
→ Owner

Technical issue:

Staff / Manager
→ Developer
→ Owner when business decision is required

---

# 10. Least Authority Principle

People should receive enough authority to perform their responsibilities without receiving unrelated sensitive authority.

Access should expand when responsibility requires it.

Not because it is convenient to give everyone administrator access.

---

# 11. Separation of Authority

Where practical, sensitive workflows should distinguish:

CREATE
REVIEW
APPROVE
PUBLISH / EXECUTE

The same person may perform multiple stages in a small company, but the stages should remain conceptually distinct.

This allows delegation later without redesigning the entire operating system.
